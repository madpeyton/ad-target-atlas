RIPK3

Ensembl gene ID: ENSG00000129465.

The product of this gene is a member of the receptor-interacting protein (RIP) family of serine/threonine protein kinases, and contains a C-terminal domain unique from other RIP family members. The encoded protein is predominantly localized to the cytoplasm, and can undergo nucleocytoplasmic shuttling dependent on novel nuclear localization and export signals. It is a component of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) receptor-I signaling complex, and can induce apoptosis and weakly activate the NF-kappaB transcription factor. [provided by RefSeq, Jul 2008].

Source: NCBI Gene summary (RefSeq), via MyGene.info, retrieved 2026-11-06.

Serine/threonine-protein kinase that activates necroptosis and apoptosis, two parallel forms of cell death (PubMed:19498109, PubMed:19524512, PubMed:19524513, PubMed:21737330, PubMed:22265413, PubMed:22265414, PubMed:22421439, PubMed:22817896, PubMed:24703947, PubMed:29883609, PubMed:31932442, PubMed:32298652, PubMed:32657447, PubMed:34811356, PubMed:40341069, PubMed:41436733). RIPK3 acts as a key mediator of necroptosis, a programmed cell death process triggered by various signaling pathways, such as tumor necrosis factor receptors (TNFRSF1A, TNFRSF1B, FAS/TNFRSF6, TNFRSF10A and TNFRSF10B), TLR3, TLR4 and/or ZBP1 (PubMed:19498109, PubMed:19524512, PubMed:19524513, PubMed:22265413, PubMed:22265414, PubMed:22421439, PubMed:24703947, PubMed:25778401, PubMed:29883609, PubMed:32298652, PubMed:32657447, PubMed:34811356). During necroptosis, activated RIPK3 forms a necrosis-inducing complex and mediates phosphorylation of MLKL, promoting MLKL localization to the plasma membrane and execution of programmed necrosis characterized by calcium influx and plasma membrane damage (PubMed:19524512, PubMed:19524513, PubMed:22265413, PubMed:22265414, PubMed:22421439, PubMed:24703947, PubMed:29883609, PubMed:31932442, PubMed:32298652, PubMed:32657447, PubMed:34811356).

RIPK3 can also phosphorylate and activate MLKL in an alternate form of necroptosis, which promotes MLKL localization to the mitochondrial membrane, leading to mitochondrial membrane damage, release of mitochondrial DNA into the cytosol and activation of the cGAS-STING pathway (By similarity). Also plays a non-catalytic role in apoptosis: apoptosis depends on RIPK1, FADD and CASP8, and is independent of MLKL and RIPK3 kinase activity (PubMed:25459880). Phosphorylates RIPK1: RIPK1 and RIPK3 undergo reciprocal auto- and trans-phosphorylation and form an amyloid signaling complex that activates necroptosis (PubMed:19524513, PubMed:22817896). In some cell types, also able to restrict viral replication by promoting cell death-independent responses (By similarity). In response to Zika virus infection in neurons, promotes a cell death-independent pathway that restricts viral replication: together with ZBP1, promotes a death-independent transcriptional program that modifies the cellular metabolism via up-regulation expression of the enzyme ACOD1/IRG1 and production of the metabolite itaconate (By similarity). Itaconate inhibits the activity of succinate dehydrogenase, generating a metabolic state in neurons that suppresses replication of viral genomes (By similarity). (Microbial infection) In case of herpes simplex virus 1/HHV-1 infection, forms heteromeric amyloid structures with HHV-1 protein RIR1/ICP6 which may inhibit RIPK3-mediated necroptosis, thereby preventing host cell death pathway and allowing viral evasion.
