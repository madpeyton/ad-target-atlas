GSTA3

Ensembl gene ID: ENSG00000174156.

Cytosolic and membrane-bound forms of glutathione S-transferase are encoded by two distinct supergene families. These enzymes are involved in cellular defense against toxic, carcinogenic, and pharmacologically active electrophilic compounds. At present, eight distinct classes of the soluble cytoplasmic mammalian glutathione S-transferases have been identified: alpha, kappa, mu, omega, pi, sigma, theta and zeta. This gene encodes a glutathione S-tranferase belonging to the alpha class genes that are located in a cluster mapped to chromosome 6. Genes of the alpha class are highly related and encode enzymes with glutathione peroxidase activity. However, during evolution, this alpha class gene diverged accumulating mutations in the active site that resulted in differences in substrate specificity and catalytic activity. The enzyme encoded by this gene catalyzes the double bond isomerization of precursors for progesterone and testosterone during the biosynthesis of steroid hormones. An additional transcript variant has been identified, but its full length sequence has not been determined. [provided by RefSeq, Jul 2008].

Glutathione S-transferase involved in the metabolism of electrophilic exogenous and endogenous compounds by glutathione conjugation, playing a role in the clearance of harmful molecules. Is involved in the detoxification pathway for aflatoxin B1-8,9-epoxide (By similarity). In vitro, has glutathione conjugating activity toward a broad range of compounds including aflatoxin B1-8,9-epoxide, 4-hydroxynonenal, trans-nonenal, 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (CDNB), 1-fluoro-2,4-dinitrobenzene, stilbene oxide, 1,2-epoxy-3-(4'-nitrophenoxy) propane, 4-chloro-3-nitrobenzaldehyde, 4-nitrophenethyl bromide and the diuretic drug ethacrynic acid.

In vitro, it also have peroxidase activity toward dilinoleoyl phosphatidylcholine hydroperoxide, beta-linoleoyl-gamma-palmitoyl-phosphatidylcholine hydroperoxide, arachidonic acid hydroperoxide, cumene hydroperoxide and tert-butyl hydroperoxide (By similarity). Catalyzes isomerization reactions that contribute to the biosynthesis of steroid hormones. Efficiently catalyzes obligatory double-bond isomerizations of delta(5)-androstene-3,17-dione and delta(5)-pregnene-3,20-dione, precursors to testosterone and progesterone, respectively (PubMed:11418619, PubMed:15595823, PubMed:20083122).
